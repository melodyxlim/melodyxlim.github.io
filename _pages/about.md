Welcome to my website. I am a Kavli Postdoctoral Fellow at Cornell University, supervised by Itai Cohen and Paul McEuen. 

I received my Ph.D. in Physics from the University of Chicago in 2022, advised by Heinrich M. Jaeger, and obtained my B.Sc. from Duke University in 2016. 

My research goal is to discover the physical principles that underpin embodied intelligent matter. When a white blood cell detects the chemical traces of a bacterial invader, it adaptively moves to track, intercept, and engulf the intruder, using just a chemical signaling network, integrated with the soft material of the cell body. In doing so, it embodies incredible physical intelligence: the ability to sense, store, and learn from information in order to express adaptive mechanical function and modify the environment. Despite unprecedented advances in our ability to design and create microscopic responsive structures, developing physically intelligent synthetic matter remains an outstanding scientific goal. What does it look like for a synthetic material to embody intelligence as an emergent, fundamental physical property? What are the basic physical principles that are necessary and sufficient for intelligent function?
